## Alloimmunisation
Production of antibodies against foreign RBC antigens belonging to an individual of other same species
#### Causes
1. Mismatched Transfusion
2. Fetomaternal bleed >0.1ml
	1. [[OBGY Notes/Third Stage of Labour\|Third Stage of Labour]]
	2. Miscarriage, MTP
	3. [[OBGY Notes/Antepartum Hemorrhage\|Antepartum Hemorrhage]]
	4. Hydatiform mole
	5. External cephalic version, Abdominal trauma
*// Antibody formation takes atleast 6 months so first baby is unaffected*
*// ABO incompatibility is protective against Rh sensitisation because its removed faster and in a way that damages Rh antigen making itnot immunogenic*

## Clinical Features
Severeity of disease may vary. Batteries not included
>[!info] Pathogenesis
>- Rh-negative mothers alloimmunised against Rh antigen
>- IgG crosses placental and attatch to Fetal RBC
>- Affected RBC removed from circulation
>- Desctruction of RBC leads to coagulopathy and Anemia
#### Hydrops Fetalis
Most severe form of Rh hemolytic disease
- Excess RBC destruction - Severe anemia, Anoxia, [[Medicine notes/Metabolic Acidosis\|Metabolic Acidosis]]
- Placental hyperplasia
- Liver damage -> Hypoproteinemia -> Generalised edema/Ascitis
- Sonography shows *Buddha position* with to halo around edematous scalp
- Death by cardiac failure
#### Icterus Gravis Neonatorum
Less severe manifestation. *Jaundice within 24 hours of delivery*
- Bilirubin is excreted into mother or Amnion
- After cord is clamped, Baby begins to develop jaundice
- Bilirubin >20mg/dl leads to kernicterus
#### Congenital Anemia of newborn
Least severe form. Hemolysis continues upto 6 weeks
- Pallor
- Hepatosplenomegaly

>[!quote] Maternal effects of Rh incompatibility
>1. [[OBGY Notes/Pre-eclampsia\|Pre-eclampsia]]
>2. [[OBGY Notes/Polyhydramnios\|Polyhydramnios]]
>3. Big baby
>4. [[OBGY Notes/Postpartum hemorrhage\|Postpartum hemorrhage]]
>5. Maternal Syndrome - Generalised Edema, Proteinuria, Pruritis

## Investigations
To be done in all Rh-negative mothers
1. Detailed obstetric history to assess risk of Alloimmunisation
2. ABO and Rh grouping and Genotyping of both parents 
3. Indirect Coombs
4. Fetal Rh status
5. IgG antibody estimation. >10IU/ml is significant 
6. Doppler, USG, [[OBGY Notes/Non-stress test\|Cardiotocography]]
7. Cord Blood bilirubin
8. Spectrophotometry of amniotic fluid for bilirubin level (Liley Zones I, II, III)

## Treatment
#### Prevention of Isoimmunisation
1. [[OBGY Notes/Anti-D immunoglobulin\|Anti-D immunoglobulin]]
2. Precautions to avoid feto-Maternal bleed during examination and delivery (Early cord clamping, Avoid spillage)

#### Delivery 
Done by Cesarean section or Amniotomy (low rupture of membranes)
*Liley Zone I* - Delivered at term (37 weeks)
*Liley Zone II* - Delivered Pre-term(>34 weeks)
*Liley Zone III* - [[OBGY Notes/Intrauterine Fetal Transfusion\|Intrauterine Fetal Transfusion]] until 34 weeks

#### [[OBGY Notes/Exchange transfusion for Newborn\|Exchange transfusion for Newborn]]
Done after birth can be lifesaving for severe Rh disease

#### Adjuvant Therapy
1. Phototherapy
2. Phenobarbitone
3. Prophylactic Antibiotics
