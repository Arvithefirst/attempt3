*Commonest Malpresentation*. Incidence about 20% at 28th week corrects to 3-4% at term

#### Types
1. *Complete (flexed breech)* - Presenting part is buttocks, external genitalia and feet
2. *Breech with extended limbs(Frank breech)* - Thighs flexed, common in Primigravid
3. *Footling presentation* - Thighs and legs partially extended. Presenting feet first
4. *Knee Presentation* - thigs extended but knees flexed

Can also be classified clinically into
1. Uncomplicated
2. Complicated - Prematurity, [[OBGY Notes/Twin Pregnancy\|Twin Pregnancy]], Contracted pelvis, [[OBGY Notes/Placenta previa\|Placenta previa]]

## Etiology
1. [[OBGY Notes/Preterm Labour\|Preterm Labor]]
2. *Factors preventing spontaneous version* - Frank breech, [[OBGY Notes/Twin Pregnancy\|Multiple Pregnancy]],[[OBGY Notes/Oligohydramnios\|Oligohydramnios]], Congenital abnormality of uterus, IUD
3. *Favourable adaptation* - Hydrocephalus, [[OBGY Notes/Placenta previa\|Placenta previa]], contracted pelvis
4. *Undue motility* - [[OBGY Notes/Polyhydramnios\|Hydramnios]], Multiparity
5. *Fetal Abnormality* - Trisomy 13, 18, 21

## Clinical features
1. Examination reveals Ballotable head in fundus and breech in pelvic grips
2. Soft irregular parts or sacrum felt per vaginally
3. Ischial tuberosities palpable during labour

#### Complications
Maternal complications are minimal, Arise due to necessary C-section
Fetal
1. Intrapartum death
2. Intracranial hemorrhage
3. Birth Asphyxia
4. Biirth trauma

## Investigations
1. Clinical examination
2. USG
	1. Confirm
	2. detect anomalies
	3. Measure engaging diameter
	4. Localise placenta
	5. Assess liquor volume
3. CT/MRI - measure Pelvic volume

## Management
1. External Cephalic Version
2. Elective Cesarean section
3. Vaginal delivery
	1. Spontaneous
	2. Assisted breech delivery
	3. Breech Extraction

### External Cephalic Version
Used in management of [[OBGY Notes/Breech Presentation\|Breech Presentation]]

Can be considered if spontaneous version fails to occur by 36 weeks. Can be aided by tocolytics. Successful about 60% of the time
#### Contraindications
1. Abnormal [[OBGY Notes/Non-stress test\|Cardiotocography]]
2. [[OBGY Notes/Antepartum Hemorrhage\|Antepartum Hemorrhage]], [[OBGY Notes/Premature Rupture of Membranes\|Premature Rupture of Membranes]]
3. Hyperextension of head, congenital abnormalities
4. [[OBGY Notes/Twin Pregnancy\|Multiple Pregnancy]]
6. Contracted pelvis
7. Obstetric complications ([[OBGY Notes/Pre-eclampsia\|Pre-eclampsia]], Obesity, Elderly primigravid)
8. [[OBGY Notes/Hemolytic disease of Newborn\|Rh Incompatibility]]

### Assisted Breech Delivery 
Done slwoly and patiently
1. Lithotomy with 15 degree lateral tilt
2. [[OBGY Notes/Episiotomy\|Episiotomy]]
3. Delivery of trunk (Frank breech delivered by forcing flexion by putting pressure in popliteal fossa)
4. Delivery of arms by fundal pressure
5. Delivery of aftercoming head 
	1. *Burns-Marshall method*
		1. Let hang by its own weight
		2. Fundal pressure on sinciput to encourage flexion of head
		3. When neck is visible use one hand to pull by ankles and the other to guard perineum. Pull in a steady circlular arc
	2. Forceps Delivery
	3. Malar flexion and shoulder traction method
