## Pathophysiology
Typically involes Rectum and spreads proximally causing *Proctitis > Left sided colitis > Pancolitis*

- *Hose Pipe Appearance*
- *Pseudopolyp formation* due to normal/hypertrophied tissue
- *Cryptitis* typically with crypt abscesses
- Dysplasia of colonic mucosa

## Clinical features
The main symptom of UC is
1. Bloody diarrhoea with mucus

// Some patients may pass small loose stool while others may be severely complicatied. *May present with Abdominal cramps, tenesmus, anorexia, malaise, fever.* Remission may be incited by stress, NSAID/antibiotic use or infections

## Treatment
Differs based on extent of disease, however, in general medical management of UC aims to 
- Induce remission
- Maintain remission
- Prevent dysplastic changes and complications

#### Acute proctitis
1. Mesalazine(Salicylate) 1g suppository *(Mainstay)*
2. 5-Aminosalicylate (5-ASA)

#### Active Left sided or extensive colitis
1. 5-ASA foam/enema
2. Oral Prednisolone
3. Calcium and Vitamin D supplement

#### Severe colitis
1. IV Glucocorticoid
2. Nutritional and fluid support
3. Ciclosporin
4. Consider surgical colectomy *(60% of all patients will require eventually)*
