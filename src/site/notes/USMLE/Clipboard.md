Abstract

#### Background Diabetic patients in India perform insufficient self-care activities. While Standard Health education interventions in India have been proven effective over multiple studies and education programs. The impact of Brochures as a medium of information dissemination hasn't been studied in rural India. Methods A Community based Interventional study was conducted at a Rural Hospital Training Center over 3 months. The posters were installed and one month later a Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices questionnaire was administered to ~100 patients who were then divided on the basis of whether they had been exposed to the poster or not. Results Exposure to Brochures was positively correlated to scores in all 3 domains of the KAP survey. However only the change in the ‘practices’ domain was statistically significant. Specifically Brochures seemed to improve Active management and Foot care amongst diabetics. Regression analysis showed that Positive Attitudes were a significant predictor of Improved Practices. Conclusions Our results show that although the brochures did not improve knowledge amongst participants, practices improved nevertheless. It was also noted that participants with more a optimistic outlook of their illness were more likely to engage in self care, indicating a need for positive reinforcement
