15 types, caused by defects in [[USMLE/Glycogen Metabolism\|Glycogen Metabolism]]. Glycogen granules in cells can be identifies by *periodic Acid-schoff* staining

## Type I - Von Gierke disease
Autosomal recessive Glucose-6-phosphate deficiency
#### Clinical features
- Severe Fasting hypoglycemia
- Increasesd glycogen in Liver kidneys -> Heepato/Renomegaly
- Increased Blood Lactate and Uric acid -> [[Medicine notes/Acute Gout\|Gout]]
- Increased Triglycerides
#### Treatment
- Frequent Oral glucose
- Avoid Galactose and Fructose

## Type II - Pompe Disease
Autosomal Recessive Lysosomal acid a1,4-glucosidase
#### Clinical Features
- Cardiomyopathy (Pompes takes Pump)
- Hypotonia, Exercise Intolerance
- Enlarged tongue and systemic findings lead to early death


## Type III - Cori Disease
Autosomal Recessive deficiency of Debranching Enzymes
#### Clinical featuress
Milder features [[USMLE/Glycogen Storage Diseases#Type I - Von Gierke disease\|Von Gierke Disease]]
- Normal Lactate levels
- Limit dextran type residue in lysosomes

## Type IV - Anderson Disease
Autosomal recessive deficiency of Branching enzymes

#### Clincal features
Most commonly presents with hepatosplenomegaly and failure to thrive
- nfantile Cirrhosis
- Muscle weakness
- Hypotonia
- Cardiopathy

## Type V - Mcardles disease
Autosomal Recessive deficiency of muscular glycogen phosphorylase 

#### Clinical Features
- Increased muscle glycogen - Painful cramps
- Myoglobinuria
- Second-Wind phenomenon
- Flat venous lactate curve with increasing ammonia on exercise
