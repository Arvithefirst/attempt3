 >[!quote] Definition
> *Aseptic dilation* of the whole or a part of the *pelvix calyceal system* of the kidney due to partial or intermittent *interruption of urine outflow*

## Causes
Diffrent for Uniiateral and bilateral hydronephroses

### Unilateral
1. Intraluminal - Due to [[Surgery Notes/Renal stones\|renal stones]] or papillary disease in [[Medicine notes/Diabetes Mellitus\|Diabetes]] / Analgesics
2. Intramural - Congenital(PUJ obstruction) or acquired(Ca. kidney, constriction, TB)
3. Extramural - Ca. Rectum, [[OBGY Notes/Carcinoma Cervix\|Ca Cervix]], Aberrant vessels, Horseshoe kidney

### Bilateral 
1. In children - [[Surgery Notes/Phimosis\|Phimosis]], meatal stenosis, Posterior urethral valve
2. In young adults - [[Surgery Notes/Urethral strictures\|Urethral strictures]], Bilateral abberant vessels
3. Older adults - [[Surgery Notes/Benign Prostate Hyperplasia\|BPH]], Contraction of neck of bladder
4. Physiological in pregnancy

## Clinical features
1. Painless enlargement of kidney
2. Dull aching pain in loin
3. h/o [[Surgery Notes/Renal stones\|Renal calculi]]
4. Oliguriam Oedema
5. Rarely hypertension/hematuria
>[!faq] Dietl's crisis (intermittent hydronephrosis)
> Commonly seen in calculus etiologies. Kidney become palpable after an attack of renal colic. Resolves in a few hours leaving behind polyuria as the stones slip

## Treatment
Hydronephroses secondary to another cause can be treated by managing the underlying condition 
### Anderson hynes operation
- For congenital PUJ obstruction(most common)
- Excision of spamsodic and redundant parts. A new pelvis is created and anastomosed in a dependant position

#### Other
1. Davis tube ureterostomy
2. Non-dismembered pyeloplasty
3. Bilateral nephrostomy
4. Laproscopic of retroperitnoeal pyeloplasty
5. Endoscopic pyelosis
