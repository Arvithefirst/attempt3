## Aetiopathogenesis
Thre are 2 theories that explain BPH
1. *Hormonal Theory* - Increasing age -> falling androgens -> Increased estrogern -> Hhyperplasia
2. *Neoplastic theory* - self explanatory

## Secondary changes in BPH
### Urethral
- Elongated, Compressed and converted into a narrow slit
### Bladder
- Hypertrophy causess think bundles called fasciculations or *trabeculations*
- In between these there are depressed areas called *sacculations*
- As pressure increases. sacculi herniate to form *diverticuli* which cause stasis
### Ureter and kidney
- Bilateral [[Surgery Notes/Hydronephrosis\|Hydronephrosis]], Hydroureter
- Renal failure

## Clinical features
*Triad of BPH is frequency, urgency, and hesitancy*
1. Frequency -  upto 5-10 times a day. Incomplete voiding may lead to cystitis
2. Urgency - Stretching of internal sphincter allows urine to trickle down into the posterior urethra causing urgency
3. Hesitancy - To urinate because attepts are unsuccessful due to obstruction

## Complications
1. [[Surgery Notes/Renal stones\|Renal stones]]
2. Diverticuli
3. UTI
4. Renal failure

## Investigations
1. Urea creatinine
2. Uroflowmetry
3. USG

## Treatment
### Medical Management
Done in patients with mild disease who have good urine flow minimal back pressure onto kidneys. Done with
1. Finasteride acetate (5a-reductase inhibitor)(anti-testosterone synthesis)
2. a-adrenergic blocker

### Surgical Management
Done when there is (i) Retention of urine (ii) High frequency of micturation (iii) Complications

#### Transurethral resection of prostate (TURP)
