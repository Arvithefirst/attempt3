Raised serum direct bilirubin due to obstructive pathology

## Aetiology
1. *Congenital* - Biliary atresia, choledochal cysts
2. *Inflammatory* - Ascending/sclerosing cholangitis
3. *Obstructive* - CBD stones, biliary stricture, parasitic infestation
4. *Neoplastic* - Carcinoma head of pancreas, Cholangiocarcinoma
5. Extrinsic comporession by lymph nodes or tumours

## Clinical features
1. Severe jaundice
2. Pruritus
3. Fever
4. Loss of weight/appetite

## Investigation
1. Serum bilirubin (Direct bilirubin >>>)
2. Serum Proteins (Albumin >3.5g%)
3. Prothrombin time
4. ALP, SGPT, SGOT
5. USG abdomen
6. [[Surgery Notes/Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography(ERCP)\|Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography(ERCP)]]ERCP
7. CT abdomen (for tumour)
8. Urine tests (fauchets and Hays)

## Treatments
>[!faq] Preoperative procedures
	1. Vitamin K, FFP preparation, Blood products
	2. Adequate hydration
	3. Neomycin, Lactulose
	4. Mannitol to prevent hepato-renal syndrome
	5. Repeated electrolyte, Ptt study
	6. ANtibiotics and. calcium supplement
### Surgerical options
Depending on etiology there are many available treatment options
#### CBD stones
[[Surgery Notes/Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography(ERCP)\|ERCP]] Stone removal, Choledocholithotomy, Transduodenal sphincteroplasty, Choledechojejunostomy, Choledochoduodenostomy
#### Carcinoma
Whipples operation, Triple bypass, [[Surgery Notes/Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography(ERCP)\|ERCP]] stenting
#### Biliary Stricture 
Stenting, Choledochojejunostomy, Roux-en-Y hepatico jejunostomy
#### Biliary atresia
Kasais operation, Liver transplant
#### Choledochal cyst
Excision
