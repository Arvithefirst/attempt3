Stone formations within the gall bladder may be
1. Cholesterol stones - Solitary, crystalline
2. Pigment stones(90%)
3. Mixed stones - Multiple, black

## Pathogenesis
#### I. Metabolic
- Suddenly increased cholesterol or inadequate bile salts/lecithin may precipitate cholesterol into bile *(lithogenic bile)* which then forms cholesterol monohydrate stones *(Admirons triangular hypothesis)*
- Crystals undergo nucleation and induce further crystallisation
>[!info] Factors affecting bile salt : Cholesterol ratio
	1. Obesity
	2. Drugs (OCP)
	3. Ileal disease/resection
	4. Altered enterohepatic circulation
#### II. Infections
can induce formation of stones typically caused by bacteria(E.coli, Salmonella)or parasites like Ascaris lumbricoides
#### III. Bile stasis
May occur due to pregnancy oestrogen therapy, Long term IV fluid therapu
#### IV. Increased Bilirubin production
Occurs in all hemolytic conditions
#### V. [[Surgery Notes/Saints Triad\|Saints Triad]]

>[!quote] Some miscelleneous signs 
>Triradiate or Biradiate radiolucent gas within stone shows *Mercedes Benz sign* or *seagull sign* respectively
>Calcium carbonate build up within gallbladder fills it with "Toothpaste-like" material called *limey gallbladder*

## Related Pathologies
Vary with location of stones
1. In gallbladder
	1. [[Surgery Notes/Silent gallstones\|Silent/asymptomatic Gallstone]]
	2. Biliary colic with periodicity
	3. [[Surgery Notes/Acute Cholecystitis\|Acute Cholecystitis]]
	4. Chronic Cholecystitis
	5. Perforation causing biliary peritonitis
	6. Mucocele gallbladder
	7. Carcinoma gallbladder
	8. Empyema gallbladder
	9. Limey gallbladder
2. In Common bile duct
	1. Secondary CBD stones
	2. Cholangitis
	3. Pancreatitis
	4. [[Mirizzi Syndrome\|Mirizzi Syndrome]]
3. In intestine
	1. Cholecystoduodenal fistula causing intestinal obstruction

## Medical Dissolution of Gallstones
Not usually preferred as its *expensive, long term, without good results and may cause hepatic dysfunction*
#### Indications
- Still functioning gallbladder
- SIngle stone <1.5cm
- Radioluscent stone
- Old age / unfit for surgery
#### Drugs used
1. Cheno-deoxycholic acid (CDCA)
2. Ursodeoxycholicacid
3. Citrates
4. Monoterpes
