## Aetiology
1. Infection
2. Hot climates(dehydration)
3. Dietary factors (red meats/high calcium foods/Low vitamin A foods)
4. Metabolic causes (hyperparathyroidism/[[Medicine notes/Acute Gout\|Gout]])
5. Immobilisation
6. Inadequate kidney drainage
7. Randalls plaques*

## Types 
### Calcium oxalate (mulberry) stones
- Hard, single, irregularm, sharp, dumbell/envelope shaped
- Early hematuria
### Uric acid stones
- Multiple, small, faceted, Radiolucent
- Respond best to lithotripsy
### Phosphate stones
- Small, round, yellow
- Forms *staghorn calculi*
### Cystine stones
- Hard, radioopaque, hexagonal
- Typically seen in pubertal girls

# Clinical features
1. Dull aching costovertebral pain. May be a/w nausea/vomitting due to stretching of capsule
2. Ureteric colic (loin to groin radiation of colicky pain)
3. [[Surgery Notes/Haematuria\|Haematuria]]
4. Recurrent UTI
5. Guarding and rigidity

## Complications
1. Calculous [[Surgery Notes/Hydronephrosis\|Hydronephrosis]]
2. Calculous pyonephroses
3. Renal failure
4. SCC

## Investigations
1. RFT
2. Plain KUB X ray
3. USG, CT abdomen
4. [[Surgery Notes/Intravenous urography\|Intravenous urography]]
5. Urine culture

## Treatment
Can be non-operative operative

### Non-operative
1. Conservative management for stones <5mm with forced diuresis
2. [[Surgery Notes/Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy(ESWL)\|Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy(ESWL)]]
3. Laser lithotripsy
 
### Operative
1. Percutaneous Nephrolithotomy
2. Open surgery (Pyelolithotomy, nephrolithotomy, Nephrectomy etc)
