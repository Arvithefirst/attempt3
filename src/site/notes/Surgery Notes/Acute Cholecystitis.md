Usually caused in impacted gallstone in Hartmanns pouch by
1. E.coli *(most common)*
2. Klebsiella
3. S.faecalis
4. Salmonella
5. Clostridium welchii *(Causes Emphysematous cholecystitis)*
*// Mode of infection* may be hematogenous or through bile duct

## Aetiopathogenesis
### Acute Calculous Cholecystitis
- Stones cause obstruction leading to *stasis and oedema of gallbladder* wall which predisposes to infection
- Impacted stons also cause *mucosal erosion. Allowing bile salts to exert toxic effects* on submucosal tissue 

### Acute Acalculus Cholecystitis
- Etiology unknown. *Usually seen in ICU patients*, or patients with extensive burns, Trauma, surgery etc

## Pathological features
1. Distended gallbladder iwth oedematous friable wall and dilated vessels
2. Areas of necrosis and patchy gangrene
3. Ulceration
4. Lumen contains infected fluid/bile/pus
5. Histology shows signs of acute inflammation

## Complications
1. *Perforation - typically in Hartmanns pouch*. Creating fistula with duodenum/intestive/biliary tree
2. Peritonitis
3. Pericholecystitic abscess
4. Cholangitis
5. Gangrenous gall bladder

## Clinical features
1. *Sudden onset pain in Rt hypochondrium* with tenderness, guarding and rigidity
2. Palpable, tender, soft gallbladder
3. Hyperaesthesia posterior side Rt 9-11th ribs *(Boas sign)*
4. Jandice, fever, nausea, tachycardia, Toxic appearance

## Investigation
1. USG abdomen
2. X-ray abdomen
3. CBC, LFT
4. HIDA / PIPIDA radioisotope study *(diagnostic)*

## Treatment
1. Advised hospitalisation
2. NG aspiration, IV fluids, analgesics, Broad spectrum antibiotics
3. Observation, Follow up USG

>[!faq] Indications for surgery ([[Surgery Notes/Cholecystectomy\|Cholecystectomy]])
	1. Empyema gallbladder
	2. Persisiting/Progressing symptoms
	3. Perforation
	4. Elderly
