Infection by parasite entamoeba histolytica. Typically found in right lobe

## Aetiopathogenesis
1. Trophozoites form *flask shaped ulcers at ileocecal and recto-sigmoid juntioncs* to enter Sup/Inf. Mesenteric veins.
2. Reach liver and usually form single absccess(70%) using *histiolysin*
3. Creates chocolate colored *"Anchovy sauce" Pus*
### Complications
Occur when the abscess ruptures into nearby areas (depends on site of abscess)
1. Into lungs *(Most common, coughs out pus, naturally resolves)*
2. Peritonitis - Requires emergency lapratomy
3. Empyema - Ruptire into pleural cavity
4. Bronchopleural fistulula - Rupture into bronchus
5. Retroperitoneal abscess - Rupture into bare area of liver
6. Amoebiasis cutis - Rupture into skin
7. Cardiac tamponade - Rupture out of left lobe *(Highest mortality)*
8. Septicemia and cirrhosis

## Clinical features
More common in *males*, in *tropical regions*, with *cirrhosis*, and a*fter suffering from amoebic dysentry*
1. Fever, weight loss, chills rigors, abdominal pain
2. Soft, tender, smooth, enlarged liver
3. Mild jaundice
4. Symptoms of complications may be present

## Investigations
1. Hemogram
2. Liver function tests
	- Widened PT, PTT
	- Altered ALP, SGPT, SGOT
3. USG abdomen to visualise cyst number location etc
4. Chest xray (complications)
5. Indirect haemagglutination
6. CT contrast
7. Sigmoidoscopy

## Treatment
Medical therapy, Aspiration, Percutaneous drainage or open surgery
#### Medical therapy
1. Metronidazole, Trinidazole
2. Cephalosporins, Tetracyclines (for secondary infections)
#### Aspiration
Done under USG guidance with *large bore needle*. Done in 6th intercostal space at mid-axillary line
#### Percutaneous drainage
Under USG guidance, *Pigtail catheter* is placed into the abscess. Fails if multiloculated. May cause bleeding and infection
#### Open surgery
Only done if repeated failure of drainage or high risk of rupture
- Transperitoneal approach, using *malechots catheter*
- Confirmation of drainage done with USG
