## Aetiology
1. Usually women between 35-70 years.
2. More common in whites, least in taiwanese 
3. Family history - Li fraumeni syndorme/BRCA I and II on chromosomes 13 and 17 
4. h/o breast cancer or benign berast diseases
5. Obesity and alcohol
6. Nulliparity, long menstruation
7. Chest radiation exposure
8. Hormone Replacement Therapy

## Classification
1. Carcinoma in situ
	1. [[Surgery Notes/Ductal carcinoma in situ(DCIS)\|Ductal carcinoma in situ(DCIS)]]
	2. Lobar carcinoma in situ(LCIS)
2. Invasive carcinoma (Foot stewarts classification)
	1. Pagets disease of nipple
	2. Invasive ductal(85%)
		1. Adenocarcinomas(80%, includes schirrous, simple and No Specific type )
		2. Medullary Carcinomas(4%)
		3. Mucinous
		4. Papillary
		5. Tubular
	3. Invasive lobular(10%)
	4. Atypical

## Clinical features
1. Bloody dicharge (in ductal carcinomas)
2. Retracted nipple
3. Peau d'orange - Blockage of intradermal lymphatics
4. Puckering of overlying skin
5. Lump

1. Local rise in temperature
2. Fixity to underlying tissue
3. Lymphadenopathy in central/pectoral/lateral/Anterior/posterior groups

## Investigations
1. CBC
2. ALP (bone/liver metastasis)
3. [[Surgery Notes/Mammogram\|Mammogram]] (BIRADS reporting)
4. FNAC
5. Trucut biopsy (vacuum assisted)
6. Incisional biopsy
7. Image guided biopsy
8. Chest Xray
9. Abdomen USG
10. Bone scan
11. Steroid hormone receptors
	1. Estrogen receptor(ER) and Progesterone receptor(PgR)
	2. [[Surgery Notes/HER-2\|HER-2/neu Receptor]]  
	3. KI-67
