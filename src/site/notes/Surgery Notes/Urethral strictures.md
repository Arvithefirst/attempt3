## Etiology
1. Congenital
2. Post-inflammatory (can be post-gonococcal or tubercular)
3. Post-instrumentation
	1. Catheter
	2. Dilatation
	3. Transurethral procedure
4. Post operative
	1. Prostatectomy
	2. Repair of ruptured uretherra
5. Rupture urethra
6. Schistosomiasis

## Clinical features
1. h/o gonococcal infection or surgery
2. Common in 20-40
3. Straning to pass urine
4. Suprapubic pain

## Investigations
1. X-ray pelvis/KUB xray
2. Ascending Urethrography (ASU)
3. Voiding cystourethrogram(CSUG)

## Treatment
inscision at 12 o'clock
1. Visual internal urethrotomy 
2. Open urethroplasty
3. Regulaar dilatation

## Complications
1. Acute retention of urine
2. Secondary stones
3. Recurrent periurethral abscess (*Watercan perineum*)
4. Recurrent epididymo-orchitis
