## Etiology
1. Typically seen in the elderly with trivial injury ([[Ortho notes/Osteoporosis\|osteoporosis]])

## Classification
There are 3 different types of classification
### Anatomical classification
The more proximal the fracture the worse the prognosis. Divided as
1. *Subcapital* just below the head
2. *Transcervical* in the middle of the neck
3. *Basal* at the base of neck

### Pauwels Classification
Based on the angle of the fracture line to the horizontal plane. The greater the angle the worse the prognosis
1. *Type 1* ~30 degrees
2. *Type 2* ~50 degrees
3. *Type 3* ~70 degrees

### Gardens classification
Based on the degree of displacement judged by the continuity of the medial trabecular stream
1. *Stage 1* - Incomplete fracture
2. *Stage 2* - Complete but undisplaced
3. *Stage 3* - Complete and partially displaced
4. *Stage 4* - Complete and fully displaced

## Clinical features
1. Mild pain in groin with inbility to move limb following trivial injury
2. External rotation of knee
3. Shortening of limb
4. Active straight leg raise not possible

>[!info] Radiological findings
>1. Break in medial cortex of neck
>2. External rotation
>3. Break in trabecular stream
>4. Break in [[Ortho notes/Shenton's line\|Shenton's line]]

## Treatment
Fragments almost never unite(*non-union*) as synovial fluid prevents effective healing. 

For an *Impacted fracture* conservative treatment may be attempted with Thomas splint (or hip spica in children)

In patients *<60 years old* reduction may be attempted and then screw fixation done

For patients *>60 years old* Hemiarthroplasty is done or the whole hip is replaced

## Complications
1. [[Ortho notes/Delayed and non-union\|Delayed and non-union]]
2. Avascualr necrosis
3. Osteoarthritis
