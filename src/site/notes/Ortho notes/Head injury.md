ConcussionOne of the m ost common causes of death in road traffic accidents

Can be classified
1. Open/closed
2. Blunt heavy
3. Simple/Complicated

## Pathophysiology
### Primary lesions
1. Diffuse neuronal damage
2. Shearing Lesions
3. Lacerations
4. Contusions
### Secondary lesoins
1. Brain swelling/ venous congestion
2. Intracranial hemorrhage
	1. Extradural hemorrhage
	2. Subdural Hemorrhage
	3. Subarachnoid hemorrhage
3. Infections

## Investiagtions
1. Detailed history of symptoms/ h/o alcohol intake
2. Vitals
3. [[Glasgow Coma Scale\|Glasgow Coma Scale]]
4. General assessment
5. CSF leakage

>[!faq] Indications for Hospitalisation
>- Altered consciousness
>- Skull fracture
>- Focal neurolocial features
>- Persistant headache, vomitting, hypertension, bradycardia
>- Bleeding
6. Xray skull
7. Serum electrolyte
8. Blood grouping
9. CT scan

## Treatment
- Protection of airway, oxygenation
- IV fluids
- Analgesics, anticonvulsants
- Diuretics
- Antibiotics

## Complications
The important SAQ part
### Early
1. Brainstem injury
2. CSF rhinorrhoea
3. Meningitis
4. Pituitary damage and endocrine failure
5. Depressed fractures

### Late
1. Chronic subdural hemorrhage
2. Early post-traumatic epilepsy
3. Late post-traumatic epilepsy
4. Post traumatic amnesia/hydrocephalus/headache








***

## Mechanism of injury
1. Distortion - Brain is soft and therefopre distorts readily. This is accentuated by its mobility in CSF allowing it experience shear forces
2. Mobility - Mobility(exaggerated in old age due to shrinking) allowsbleeding into spaces
3. Skull anatomy - Brain structure overlying sharper edges of skull are more likely to experience injury (eg over ant. cranial fossa)
4. Counter-coup injuries
