Also known as "developmental dysplasia of hip." *It is the spontaneous dislocation of hip before, during ort shortly after birth*

## Aetiology
1. Hereditary
2. Hormonal action of maternal "relaxin"
3. Breech presentation

## Pathology
Classically the presentation is with dislocated joint, but may also present with dislocat-able hip
1. Upwawrd and lateral dislocation of femoral head 
2. Shallow acetabulum
3. Hypertrophied ligamentum teres
4. Inverted limbus
5. Stretched capsule of hip jont
6. Adaptive shortening of hip muscles specifically adductors

## Clinical features
Detrected at birth usually in routine screening. As the child grows may develop abnormal creases arpund gropin and eventually a peculiar gate
- *Barlows test*
- *Ortolanis test*
- Higher buttock fold on affected side
- Galeazzis sign
- Trendelenburg sign positive, Lordosis, Telescopy positive
- Delayed appearance of ossification centres of femur
- Break in sheltons line

## Treatment
Includes methods of reduction + casting and also surgical osteotomies

### Reduction with casting
May be reduced by closed/open manipulation with or without traction. Held together by any of
- Frog leg cast
- Bachelor cast
- Van rosens splint

### Surgical procedures
- Salters osteotomy
- Charis pelvic displacement osteotomy
- Pembertons pericapsular osteotomy
