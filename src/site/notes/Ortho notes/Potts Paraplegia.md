[[Ortho notes/TB spine\|TB spine]] with neurological involvement
Happens most commonly in TB of the dorsal spine because the canal is narrowest

#### Pathology
Compression fo neural tissue due to 
1. Imflammatory oedema
2. Exrta-dural pus
3. Sequestra (Devascularised eroded bone)
4. *"Internal Gibbous"* - Angulation of deceased spine
Can also be classified as *Early onse*t or *Late onset*


## Clinical Features
1. Muscle weakness, Spasticity, Incoordination
2. Paraplegia in extension
3. Paraplegia in flexion
4. Complete flaccid Paraplegia

#### Grade of Potts Paraplegia
*Grade I* - Unaware of neural deficit. babinksi positive, Patellar clonus
*Grade II* - c/o Clumsiness. Able to walk withn or without support
*Grade III* - Not able to walk. May have extension paraplegia
*Grade IV* - Unable to walk. Sevree flexion paraplegia and loss of sensation

## Treatment
Conservative management - ATT, Monitoring, Care
### Surgical intervention
Done if not getting better, if getting worse, if recurrent, if painful or if complicated

#### Costotraversectomy
- Rib and transverseprocess removed. Fluid drained
#### Anterolateral Decompression
- Most common
- Rib, Transverse process, Pedicle and part of body removed
- Caseating material, spurs etc cleaned. out
#### Radical debridement and Arthrodesis (Hong Kong operation)
- All affected vertebrae are removed and replaced with rib grafts
