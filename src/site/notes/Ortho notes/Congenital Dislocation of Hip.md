Also called Developmental Dysplasia of hip

## Etiology
Less common in India because we carry our babies the rigth way \#represent
1. Hereditary predisposition
2. Hormone related laxity of joints
3. Breech malposition

## Pathology
Can occur at either i) birth(Classic) or ii)after birth. Changes are
1. Upward and lateral dislocation of femoral head with anteverion
2. Shallow acetabulum
3. Hypertrophied ligamentum teres
4. Adaptive shortening of hip muscles

## Clinical features
1. Usually noticed at birth by screening
2. Asymmetric groin creases, click sound, peculiar gait

>[!summary] Examination findings
>1. Barlows +ve
>2. Ortolanis +ve
>3. Galeazzis sign
>4. Trendelenburg test +ve
>5. Telescopy +ve

## Investigations
1. Xray hip(van rosens view)
	1. Hard in childern because not yet ossified head of femur
	2. Delayed ossification
	3. break in [[Ortho notes/Shenton's line\|Shenton's line]]

## Treatment
Treatment can be pushed upto 8years in b/l cases and 10- years in unilateral ones
1. Closed manipulation under GA in young kids 
2. Traction with closed manipulation
3. Open reduction

#### Surgical Methods
1. *Salter Osteotomy* - Wedge of iliac bone above acetabulkum taken out. The acetabulum is then rotated along to cover femur
2. *Chiaris Osteotomy* - Transverse division and the lower fragment displaced medially
3. *Pembertons Osteotomy* - Curved wedge taken above acetabulum
