Describes a clinical syndrome where the *heart fails to provide sufficient circulation for adequate oxygen perfusion*

## Classification
Can be classified on the basis of affected side or ejection fraction or direction of failure

### Classification by Ejection Fraction
EF could be either reduced or preserved in systolic and diastolic dysfunction respectively. Classification of aetiology and management

#### Heart Failure with reduced Ejection Fraction $(HF_rEF)$
Caused by *systolic dysfunction* resulting in. EF < 40%. Causes include
1. Decreased ventricular contractility - MI, Myocarditis, HOCM
2. Ventricular outflow obstruction - HTN, AS
3. Ventricular volume overload - MR, AR
4. Arrhythmias
Usually associated with male gender, [[Medicine notes/Acute Coronary Syndrome\|MI]], Dilated LV, and [[Third heart sound\|Third heart sound]]
#### Heart Failure with preserved Ejection Fraction $(HF_pEF)$
Caused by *diastolic dysfunction* resulting in EF > 50%. Causes include
1. Ventricular inflow obstruction - MS, Trigeminal stenosis
2. General inflow obstruction - Constrictive Pericarditis, Cardiac tamponade, Left ventricular hypertrophy
Usually associated with female gender, Lifestyle diseases, Concentric LVH and [[Fourth heart sound\|Fourth heart sound]]

### Classification by Affected side
Can be either Left, right sided or biventricular. Classification of signs

#### Left sided Heart Failure
- Pulmonary oedema *(predominant presenting sign)*
- Tachypnea, Tachycardia
- 3rd Heart sound
- [[Medicine notes/Pulsus Alterans\|Pulsus Alterans]]
#### Right sided Heart Failure
- Peripheral oedema
- Abdominal discomfort, Raised JVP, signs of portal hypertension
- Hypotension, Signs of venous pooling

### Classification by direction
Can be forward or backwards heart failure. Classification of complications

#### Forward failure
- Failing ventricular output leads to decreased renal perfusion causes Water and $Na^+$ retention through activation of RAAS 
- Leads to increased pre-load and exaggeration of symptoms
#### Backwards failure
- Failing ventricular filling leads to increased back pressure
- Causes venous pooling/portal hypertension followed by LVH in RVF
- Causes Pulmonary oedema followed by RVH in LVF

>[!info] Neurohumoural Compensatory Mechanisms in Heart failure
	1. Reduced cardiac output activates SNS and RAAS
	2. SNS and RAAS induce vasoconstriction to return blood to heart
		- Increased After-load increases damage to heart
	3. RAAS activation induces Water and Sodium Retention
		- Increased Fluid volume(preload) increases damage to heart

## Investigations
1. Hemogram, LFT, Urea, Creatinine, Electrolytes, Cardiac enzymes(*B-type Natriuretic Peptide*, Troponins, TNF receptors)
2. Chest X-ray - shows pleural oedema, *Kerley B lines*, Bat wing appearance, Cardiomegally
3. ECG - Diagnose *previous MI*, Valvular heart diseases, Ventricular hypertrophy etc. Stress ECG fro myometrial viability
4. *Echocardiogram - Assess etiology*(systolic/diastolic failure), valvular function, chamber sizes etc
5. Cardiac MRI, Radionuclide study
6. Cardiac catheterisation, Biopsy

## Complications
1. Renal Failure *(Cardiorenal syndrome)*
2. Hypokalemia (or hyper due to therapy)
3. Hyponatremia
4. Hepatic dysfunction
5. Thromboembolism
6. Arrhythmias
