Most common cause of Angina and [[Medicine notes/Acute Coronary Syndrome\|acute coronary syndrome]] in the world. More common in men, more common in developed countries, more common in older individuals.

Clinically manifests as: 
1. [[Medicine notes/Stable Angina\|Stable Angina]]
2. Unstable Angina
3. [[Medicine notes/Acute Coronary Syndrome\|Myocardial Infarction]]
4. Heart Failure
5. Arrythmia

# Pathophysiology
Usually causes by artherosclerotic plaques but may also be due to aortitis or connective tissue disorders

- [*]  Artherosclerosis is a progressive inflammatory disorder of the artery wall characterised by focal lipid rich deposits

Although symptoms begin >6 decades of life, lesions can be seen as early as the 1st decade of life.

Plaques vulnerable to thrombosis are characterised by a lipid-rich core, thin fibrocellular cap, speckled calcifications and increased inflammatory cells

Fissure are most likely along areas of high mechanical stress and may be triggered by sudden surges in BP(exercise, stress. etx)

Artherosclerosis ultimately leads to arterial remodelling

# Risk Factors
1. Age and Sex
2. Genetics
3. Smoking, Alcohol, Diet, Exercise
4. Hypertension, [[Medicine notes/Diabetes Mellitus\|Diabetes]], Hypercholestrolaemia
5. Hemostatic conditions with high plasma fibrinogen
6. Personality and social deprivation
