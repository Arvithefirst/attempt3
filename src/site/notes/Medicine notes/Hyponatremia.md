Defined as Serum Sodium <135mmol/L. Mild upto 130, moderate till 125, and severe below that

## Classification
Classified based on asociated ECF changes
### Hyponatremia with hypovolemia
Describes a situation where thre is sodium AND water deficit
#### Causes
- Renal Sodium loss (diuretics, adrenocortical failure)
- GIT sodium loss (Vomitting diarrhoea)
- Skin sodium loss (burns)

### Hyponatemia with Euvolemia
Describes a situation where the patient is clinically euvolemic usually due to increased water intake(Polydipisa, IV dextrose etc)
#### Causes
- Primary polydipsia
- Excess electrolyte free water infusion (Bladder irrigation post prostatectomy)
- [[Medicine notes/Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone Secretion\|Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone Secretion]]
- Hypothyroidism

### Hyponatremia with Hypervolemia
Descrribes a situation where excess water retention leads to volume expansion and hyponatremia
#### Causes
- Congestive cardiac failure
- Cirrhosis
- [[Medicine notes/Nephrotic Syndrome\|Nephrotic Syndrome]]
- [[Medicine notes/Chronic Kidney Disease\|Chronic Kidney Disease]]

## Investigations
1. Serum electrolytes
2. Serum macromolecules(urea, glusose lipids, Igs)*
3. Urine osmolality
4. RFTs
*// Macromolecules can cause artefactual hyponatremia*

## Management
Rapid onset(<48hrs) hyponatremia can be corrected rapidly by 3% hypertonic solution 150ml bolus over 20 minutes

Slower onset hyponatremia cannot be treated this way as brain cells adapt to the changes in plasma osmolality and rapid increase would cause further contracture and myelinolysis. *Rate of correction should not exceed 10mmol/L/day*

Hypovolemic patients should receive treatment to control the loss and fluid therapy

Euvolemic patients benefit from fluid restriction. SIADH responds well to oral Urea therapy

Hypervolemic patients should get strict fluid restriction
