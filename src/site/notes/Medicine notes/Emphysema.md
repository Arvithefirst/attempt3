Chronic lung disease characterised by *abnormal permanent dilation of airspaces distal to the terminal bronchial*, typically associated with obvious fibrosis

## Types
Classified based on where the dilation is relative to acini
1. Centroacinar - Common in *upper lobes* and apex. Associated with *smoking*, chronic bronchitis and coal workers pneumonia
2. Panacinar - Common in *lower lobes*. Associated with *$a_1$-antitrypsin deficiency*
3. Distal acinar - Common in *periphery*. Associated with *areas of scarring*/fibrosis
4. Irregular - Usually *asymptomatic*. Typically seen around healed scars(TB)

>[!faq] $a_1$-antitrypsin deficiency
>Autosomal recessive condition. Lack of antitrypisin -> increased proteolytic adctivity -> destruction of alveolar walls

## Clinical features
1. Dyspnea
2. Cough and scanty mucoid expectoration
3. Weight loss, weakness, kethargy

1. Tachypneic with use of accessory respiratory muscles
2. Pink puffer appearance with Barrel shaped chest
>[!summary] Named signs of Emphysema
>1. Campbells sign - Exaggerated descent of trachea
>2. Dahls sign - Hyperpigmentation above knees
>3. Hoovers sign - Paradoxical blunting of subcostal angle in inspiration
>4. Harrisons sign - Horizontal groove where diaphram meets ribs
3. Hyperresonant percussion
4. Flat diaphram, tubular heart, loss of peripheral markings
5. Decreased $FEV_1$ FVC. Increased TLC and RV

## Investigations
1. Chest Xray
2. Pulmonary function tests
3. ABG
4. Exercise tolerancve tests
5. ECG
#### BODE Index
2 year mortality index that acounts for BMI, Obstructive deferct severity, dyspnea and exercise tolerance

## Treatment
Non-specific
1. Bronchodilators and Corticosteroids
2. Treatment of infections and avoidance of further risk factors
3. Surgical lung volume removal to correct hyperinflation
