Medicla emergency and complication typically seen in DM type 1. 

Risk factors for which are
1. Infections
2. Insufficient insulin
3. Severe stress hyperthyroidism
4. Pregnancy
5. Infarction

### Pathogenesis
- Insulin deficiency triggers lipolysis of lipid into Free-Fatty Acids(FFAs.)\
- Inability of the liver to fully metabolise the FFAs leads to formation of Ketone bodies 

## Clinical features
1. Polyuria, Thirst
2. Weight loss
3. Weakness
4. Vomitting, weight loss, abdominal pain
5. Leg cramps

1. Signs of dehydration (sunken eyes, Tachycardia, hypotension)
2. Kussmaul breathing
3. Hypothermia
4. "fruity" breath
5. Delirium, drowsiness, coma

## Investigations
>[!summary] Cardinal features of Diabetic ketoacidosis
>1. Hyperglycemia >240mg/dl 
>2. Hyperketonemia >3mmol/L or Urine ketones +2(by dipstick)
>3. Metabolic acidosis $HCO^3$ <15mmol/L or pH <7.3

Routine investigations are also to be done


## Management
Aims to correct dehydration, Hyperglycemia, Acid-base imbalance and Acidemia
### Fluid resuscitation
Hall mark of treatment. Also corrects hyperglycemia, pH to some extent by diluting the blood.

2 litres in 1st hour
1 litre in 2nd hour
1 litre over next 2 hours
1 litre every 4 hours hence

2. Insluin 0.1 IU/Kg/Hr
3. KCL diluted in NS
4. Bicarb for acid base
