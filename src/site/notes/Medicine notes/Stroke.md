Also *Cerebrovascular Accident(CVA)*. It is acute neurological injury as a result of infarction or hemorrhage

## Classification
*Based on pathophysiology* they can be divided into 
1. [[Medicine notes/Ischaemic stroke\|Ischaemic stroke]] (80%)
	- Large vessel infarcts
	- Small Vessel infarcts
	- Cardiac emboli
2. Hemorrhagic Stroke(20%)
	- Hypertensive Bleeds
	- Anmyloid angiopathy
	- Vascular malformation
	- Non traumatic [[Subarachnoid Hemorrhage\|Subarachnoid Hemorrhage]]

*Based on duration and evolution of symptoms* they can be divided into
1. Complete stroke
2. Evolving stroke
3. [[Transient Ischaemic attack\|Transient Ischaemic attack]]
4. Reversible Ischaemic neurological defect

## Investigations
1. CT/MRI to properly classify stroke
2. Echocardiogram (*if suspected cardiac embolus*)
3. CBC, BSL, Lipid Profile (*Risk Factors*)
4. ESR, Clotting factors, Drug screen, Genetic studies etc

## Treatment
Neuroimaging must be done to identify etiology of stroke

For ischaemic stroke
- Assess if *thrombolysis or mechanical thrombectomy* is possible and do it
- Otherwise prescribe aspirin and shift to Acute srtoke unit
For Hemorrhagic stroke
- *Reverse coagulation abnormalit*y and shift to Acute Stroke unit

Supportive treatment
1. Secure airway, ventilation
2. Correct circulation abnormalities (hypoperfusion, arrythmias, BP)
3. aintain fluid-electrolyte balance
4. Monitor BSL, avoid Hypoglycemia
5. Control Pyrexia
6. Check and treat incontinence

## Complications
1. Epileptic seizures
2. Depression
3. Painful shoulder
4. Chest infection
5. Constipation
6. Urinary Tract infection
7. Pressure sores
8. DVT

## Prevention
Lifestyle changes and hypertension therapy has to given to all patients of stroke. BP control is especially important in hemorrhagic stroke

For Ischaemic stroke
- Statins to lower cholesterol
- If ECG reveals atrial fibrillation then consider Anticoagulants
- If sinus rhythm then start anti-platelet Therapy
- If carotid stenosis the Carotidendartectomy followed by antiplatelet therapy
