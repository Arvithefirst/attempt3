Can occur due to disease of the aortic valve, cusps, infection of dilation of the aortic root.

# Causes
1. Congenital bicuspid valve
2. <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Rheumatic disease</mark>
3. <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">Infective endocarditis</mark>
4. Trauma 
5. <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Aortic dilation</mark>
	1. Marfans
	2. Aneurysm
	3. Aortic dissection
	4. Syphillis
	5. Ankylosing spondylosis

# Clinical Features of Aortic Regurgitation
Signs
1. <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Palpitations</mark>
2. Breathlessness
3. Angina

 Symptoms
1. <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Collapsing "Water hammer" pulse</mark>
2. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Bounding "Dancing" Carotids (Corrigans sign)</mark>
3. <mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;">Capillary pulsation on nail bed (Quincke sign)</mark>
4. Femoral bruit (Duroziez sign)
5. Head nodding with pulse (De mussets sign)
6. Systolic murmur (Increased Stroke volume)
7. <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Austin Flint murmur (soft - mid diastolic)</mark>
8. Decreased diastolic BP, Wide pulse pressure

# Investigations
1. ECG - Left Ventricular Hypertrophy, Inverted T-wave
2. Chest X-ray - Dilation, features of Left-sided Heart Failure
3. <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Doppler Electrocardiography</mark> - Dilated, hyperdynamic LV

# Treatment
- Treat underlying infection
- Aortic valve replacement (if end systolic volume > 55mm)
- Aortic root replacement (For root dilation etiology)

Patiets of chronic Aortic regurgitation may remain asymptomatic for many years due to compensatory mechanisms (Ventricular dilation/hypertrophy.) -> Leads to increased BP.
