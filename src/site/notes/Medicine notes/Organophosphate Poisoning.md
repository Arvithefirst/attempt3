Typically due to accidental exposure to pesticides. OP may also be used in chemical warfare. *Organophosphates are Competitive Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors*

## Clinical Symptoms
OPs potentiate Cholinergic actions by inhibiting AChE's. Affects Muscarininc, Nicotinic and CNS receptors of Acetylcholine 

| Muscarinic     | Nicotinic      | CNS                    |
| -------------- | -------------- | ---------------------- |
| *D*iarrhoea    | Weakness       | Fatigue                |
| *U*rination    | Fasciculations | Confusion              |
| *M*iosis       | Paralysis      | Unconsciousness        |
| *B*ronchorrhea |                | Seizure                |
| *B*ronchospasm | Hypertension   | Ataxia                 |
| *E*mesis       | Tachycardia    | Respiratory Depression |
| *L*acrimation  |                |                        |
| *S*alivation   |                |                        |
| *S*weating     |                |                        |

## Investigations
Medical emergency diagnosed clinically. After stabilisation, A RBC cholinesterase activity test can be done
 
## Treatment
1. Strip naked and Lavage (To avoid further exposure)
2. Supportive ventilation/intubation. Benzodiazepines/sedatives if agitated
3. Atropine 2mg IV doubled every 5-10 minutes or until *Atropinization*
4. Pralidoxime

>[!faq] Sequelae of OP poisoning
>**Intermediate Syndrome**
>1-4 days after exposure, 20% of patients develop rapid weakness of proximal muscles causing respiratory failure. May last 2-3 weeks. No specific treatment besides supportive therapy
>
>*Organophosphate Induced Delayed Polyneuropathy (OPIDP)*
>Rarely patients develop mixed motor/sensory polyneuropathy 2-3 weeks post exposure leading to flaccid paralysis, wasting, parasthesia etc. Regular physiotherapy may limit muscle wasting. Substantial recovery is possible in young patients but is often incomplete
