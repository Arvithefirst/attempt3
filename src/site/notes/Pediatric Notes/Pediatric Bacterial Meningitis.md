More common in neonates because of poor immunity and phagocytic function

## Etiology
Below 3 months - E.coli, Strep. pneumoniae, Salmonella spp
3 months to 3 years - Haemophilus influenzae, S.pneiumoniae, Meningococci
Above 3 years - S.pneumoniae, N. Meningitidis

## Clinical features 
Same as [[Medicine notes/Bacterial Meningitis\|Bacterial Meningitis]]
1. Fever
2. [[Medicine notes/Meningitis#^c0695d\|Meningism]]
3. Seizures
4. Cheynes stokes breathing

In Young infants neck rigidity and Kernigs are rare. Instead look for
1. Sepsis
2. Vacant stare, refusal to feed, Poor cry
3. Alternating irritability and drowsiness
4. Persistant vomitting and fever
5. Shock, tremors

#### Meningococcal Meningitis
- Typically by serotype A
- Also show petechial hemorrhages
- May present with acute fulminant illness of adrenal insufficiency, hypotension, shoch and coma called *Waterhouse Friderichsen Syndrome*
#### Tubercular Meningitis
- Its own short note
#### Pneumococcal meningitis
- Incommon in first few months
- usually follow otitis media, Injury
#### Staphylococcal meningitis
- Usually follows umbilical supsis in young
- Or otitis media, Mastoiditis, Scalp lesions in older children
#### HiB meningitis
- Frequent between 3 and 12 months
- Focal sigs and fever persist after CSF is cleared
- Residual auditory deficit is common

## Investigations
1. CSF tap
2. CT head
3. RDT(latex agglutination, ELISA)

## Treatment
Empirical treatment with 3rd generation cephalosporin or Ampicillin + Chloramphenicol

Meninhococcal - Penicillin/ Cefotaxime
HiB - Ceftriaxone
Staph - Penicillin < Methicillin < Vancomycin
Listeria - Ampicillin + Aminoglyuvoside

#### Symptomatic treatment
- Osmotic diuretics
- Anticonvulsants
- IV and maintenance fluids to correct electrolyte imbalance and avoid shock
- Proper nursing and hygeine

## Complications
1. Subdural empyema
2. Hydrocephalus
